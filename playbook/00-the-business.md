# The business, honestly

## What this actually is

You sell **web accessibility audits and remediation** to organisations whose
websites fail WCAG. The scan is automated (this repo). The money is in the
report, the conversation it starts, and the fix work that follows.

This is not passive income. It is a service business with an unusually good
shape: near-zero cost per prospect, a deliverable that proves its own value
before anyone pays, and a buyer with a deadline.

## Why this one works when most "make money" ideas don't

Four things have to be true for a service business to start fast. This has all four.

1. **The problem is verifiable, not hypothetical.** You are not claiming a
   prospect *might* have a problem. You hand them a document listing the exact
   elements on their exact site that fail, with WCAG references. Nobody argues
   with a screenshot of their own broken form.
2. **The buyer already has budget.** Accessibility remediation comes out of
   legal-risk, web, or compliance budget — all of which exist already. You are
   redirecting spend, not creating it.
3. **Cost to produce a lead magnet is effectively zero.** A scan costs you a
   few minutes of compute. You can send 200 genuinely personalised, genuinely
   useful reports without spending money.
4. **There is a deadline.** See below.

## The deadline that makes this urgent right now

In April 2024 the US Department of Justice issued a final rule under **ADA
Title II** requiring state and local government entities to meet **WCAG 2.1
Level AA**. Compliance deadlines run on entity size — the larger tier landed
first, smaller entities follow roughly a year later.

That covers a very large, very findable population of buyers:

- City and county governments
- School districts, community colleges, state universities
- Public libraries and library systems
- Transit authorities, utilities, park districts
- Courts and other public agencies

These organisations have a legal obligation with a date attached, public
procurement processes, published contact details, and websites that are very
often non-compliant. That is close to an ideal cold-outreach target.

Separately, private businesses face **ADA Title III** exposure. Thousands of
web accessibility lawsuits and far more pre-litigation demand letters are filed
in the US each year, concentrated in retail, food service, hospitality, and
healthcare.

> **Verify the current deadlines yourself before you cite them to a prospect.**
> Rules and dates change. Link prospects to ada.gov rather than paraphrasing
> the law, and never tell anyone they *are* or *are not* legally compliant —
> you are a technologist, not their lawyer. See `legal-and-ethics.md`.

## Unit economics

| | |
|---|---|
| Cost per scan | ~$0 (your compute) |
| Time per scan | 1–3 minutes, unattended |
| Time to review + personalise a report | 10–15 minutes |
| Audit sold standalone | $500 – $2,500 |
| Remediation project | $2,000 – $15,000 |
| Monitoring retainer | $150 – $600 / month |

The retainer is the part that compounds. One-off audits pay this month;
retainers are what make month six better than month one.

## What the realistic funnel looks like

These are planning numbers for a beginner with no reputation, no case studies,
and a cold list. They are deliberately pessimistic.

```
200  scans run                    (a weekend of unattended compute)
120  worth sending  (60%)         — the rest scored too well to be worth a pitch
 12  replies         (10%)        — free report, personalised, no pitch
  5  calls booked    (40% of replies)
  1–2 closed         (25–35% of calls)
```

**One to two clients in month one.** At a $1,500 audit-plus-fixes package,
that is $1,500–$3,000. It is not life-changing money. It is a real first
customer, a case study, and a reference — and those are what make month two
land at a materially higher number.

Anyone promising you more than that from a standing start is selling you
something.

## Where it goes wrong

- **You send 20 emails and stop.** The maths above needs volume. Twenty
  prospects is not a test of anything.
- **You lead with the pitch instead of the report.** The report is the reason
  anyone replies. Attach it, ask nothing, and let it work.
- **You sell "compliance".** You cannot deliver compliance and you should never
  claim to. You deliver *specific fixes to specific defects*.
- **You take a remediation job you cannot do.** If you cannot write HTML/CSS,
  sell the audit and subcontract the fixes. Quoting work you cannot deliver is
  how a first client becomes a refund.
- **You quote from the tool's estimate without reading the findings.** The
  estimate is a starting point produced by a heuristic. Read the report before
  you put a number in front of a human.
