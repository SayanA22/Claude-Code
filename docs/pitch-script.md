# Itemize — 5-minute pitch script

**Target run time: 4:45.** The limit is 5:00 and going over is a disqualification risk, so
leave the buffer. Roughly 700 spoken words. Normal playback speed is required — you cannot
speed the video up to fit.

The single biggest advantage in this pitch is that **the product works**. Two full minutes
are spent screen-recording it. Do not turn those two minutes into slides.

| Time | Section | On screen |
|------|---------|-----------|
| 0:00–0:35 | The bill | The itemized bill, filling the frame |
| 0:35–1:05 | Why nobody fights it | Stats, then the advocate's fee |
| 1:05–1:20 | The idea | Product name and one line |
| 1:20–2:40 | **Live demo** | Screen recording of the app |
| 2:40–3:20 | Value innovation | Strategy canvas, then ERRC grid |
| 3:20–3:50 | Noncustomers | Three tiers diagram |
| 3:50–4:25 | Business model | Revenue lines + unit economics |
| 4:25–4:45 | Feasibility and close | Prototype URL on screen |

---

## 0:00 — The bill *(35s)*

> This is a real kind of hospital bill. Eleven lines, five-digit codes, six thousand
> six hundred and nine dollars.
>
> Line two is an ankle X-ray for six hundred and twelve dollars. Line three is the same
> X-ray, again, on the same day. Line four is twenty units of a drug that's given in one.
> Line eight is a doctor you never chose, billing you separately — which federal law
> actually forbids.
>
> I can tell you all of that. You can't, and neither can the person who got this bill.
> So they pay it.

*Point at each line as you say it. Let the frame sit on the paper. Do not rush this.*

## 0:35 — Why nobody fights it *(30s)*

> More than a hundred million Americans are carrying medical debt they can't pay — two
> hundred and twenty billion dollars of it. About half of adults couldn't cover a surprise
> five-hundred-dollar bill.
>
> There are people who fix bills. They're called billing advocates, and they're good.
> They also take twenty-five to thirty-five percent of whatever they save you, or up to
> three hundred and fifty dollars an hour — so they won't touch a bill under a few
> thousand dollars. Which means the bills most people actually get, nobody will look at.

## 1:05 — The idea *(15s)*

> Itemize is a line-by-line audit of a hospital bill that takes three minutes, works on
> a six-hundred-dollar bill, costs nothing, and writes the appeal letter for you.
>
> Here it is. This is running.

## 1:20 — Demo *(80s — the heart of the pitch)*

Screen-record this exact sequence. Narrate over it; don't describe your own clicks.

1. **The bill loads flagged.** "Every questionable line is marked before I touch anything.
   Thirty-five hundred dollars of a sixty-six-hundred-dollar bill is in question."
2. **Open finding 1 — the duplicate.** "Same code, same day. The app tells you what to say:
   *send me the order and report for each one, or remove the duplicate*."
3. **Open finding 3 — the unbundled panel.** "It billed a metabolic panel and then billed
   two of the tests inside that panel separately. Medicare's own coding rules forbid that."
4. **Open a price finding.** "This X-ray is fifteen times the Medicare rate. Since 2021
   every hospital has been required to publish its own cash price — so this one's negotiable,
   and the app says exactly how to ask."
5. **The coverage toggle — the money shot.** Switch *Uninsured* to *Insured*. "Watch the
   argument change. This patient is now insured, so the out-of-network ER doctor's bill
   isn't a negotiation any more — the No Surprises Act voids it. Same bill, different law,
   because the engine knows which one applies to you."
6. **Click "Write my appeal packet".** "And there's the letter. Every objection numbered,
   with the rule it rests on, a records request, and a hold on collections. Print it, sign
   it, send it."

## 2:40 — Value innovation *(40s)*

> Here's the strategy canvas. Advocates compete on expertise — and expertise is a person,
> which is why the fee is high and the small bills get refused. The status quo competes
> on nothing.
>
> Itemize eliminates the contingency fee, the intake call, and the records handover.
> It raises legal coverage, price transparency, and evidence — every claim carries the
> rule it rests on. And it creates something that didn't exist: an audit worth running on
> a six-hundred-dollar bill.
>
> That's differentiation and low cost at the same time. Not a cheaper advocate — a
> different thing.

## 3:20 — Noncustomers *(30s)*

> The market isn't advocacy customers. It's three tiers of people who aren't anyone's
> customer. The ones who suspect the bill is wrong and give up. The ones an advocate turns
> away because their bill is too small to be worth a fee. And the biggest tier — people who
> never knew a hospital bill could be wrong at all, whose legal rights quietly expire.

## 3:50 — Business model *(35s)*

> The audit is free permanently, and that's deliberate — charging a share of savings is
> the incumbent's model and it's the thing I'm eliminating.
>
> Revenue comes from things with real cost: nine dollars to mail the packet certified with
> proof of delivery; fifteen dollars to file the federal dispute for you. And the line that
> scales — forty cents per member per month from self-insured employers, who pay their own
> employees' claims and save money when those claims get audited.
>
> The audit itself runs in your browser. No server, no AI inference, no staff. One audit
> and a million audits cost the same to serve. That's why free is sustainable.

## 4:25 — Feasibility and close *(20s)*

> None of this needs inventing. The coding rules, the Medicare rates, and every hospital's
> published prices are all free public data. The engine is deterministic rules, not a
> language model — which is why it's auditable, and why it costs nothing per run.
>
> A hundred million people are being asked to pay bills nobody will read for them.
> Itemize reads it in three minutes, for free, and hands them the letter.
>
> Try it yourself. Thank you.

---

## Delivery notes

- **Say numbers as words in the script, but show them on screen.** Judges retain the visual.
- **The demo must be a real screen recording**, not a slide of a screenshot. A working
  prototype is the strongest thing you have against the *technical feasibility* criterion.
- Do not read this script flatly off the page. Learn the beats, then talk.
- If you are over time, cut from 3:20–3:50 (noncustomers) first — the canvas already
  implies it. Never cut the demo.
- Record the demo at 1280×800 or larger so the bill text is legible after compression.
