The visual versions live in the strategy pack page (`app/strategy.html`). This file is the
text of record — use it to build slides, or to answer a judge's follow-up.

## 1. Strategy canvas

Offering level 0–10 across the factors this industry competes on. On *cost to the patient*,
a **low** line is the winning position; on every other factor, high wins.

| Competing factor | Patient advocacy firms | Status quo (portals, billing office, DIY) | Itemize |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost to the patient | 8.0 | 1.0 | **0.5** |
| Speed to first answer | 3.0 | 2.0 | **9.5** |
| Coding-error detection | 9.0 | 1.5 | **8.0** |
| Price benchmarking | 6.0 | 1.0 | **9.0** |
| Legal rights covered | 7.0 | 2.0 | **9.0** |
| Paperwork written for you | 8.0 | 1.0 | **9.5** |
| Works on small bills | 1.0 | 3.0 | **9.5** |
| Privacy of your records | 3.0 | 4.0 | **10.0** |

The shape of the argument: advocacy firms score high on detection *because a human does the
work*, which is exactly what forces the fee up and the minimum bill size with it. Itemize
gets most of that detection from deterministic rules over public data, so the cost line can
drop to the floor instead of trading against it.

## 2. Eliminate–Reduce–Raise–Create grid

**Eliminate**
- The contingency fee — no 25–35% of savings
- Case intake: the consultation call, the case manager, the queue
- The records handover — no account, no upload, no third party holding medical history
- The minimum bill size below which no advocate will take the case

**Reduce**
- Time to a first answer: weeks of back-and-forth → about three minutes
- Documents the patient must gather: the itemized bill alone starts the audit
- Jargon: every finding is written as the sentence you would say on the phone
- Marginal cost per audit: to effectively zero, which is what makes "free" durable

**Raise**
- Legal coverage: No Surprises Act, good faith estimates, ACA preventive rules and price
  transparency, all applied to one bill at once
- Price transparency: every coded line benchmarked against Medicare, multiple stated aloud
- Evidence: each objection carries the rule it rests on
- Privacy: the audit runs in the browser; no bill reaches a server

**Create**
- An instant line-by-line verdict with a dollar figure attached to every objection
- The appeal packet — letter and phone script — written before you have decided to fight
- A rights engine that follows the patient: change coverage and the governing law changes
- An audit worth running on a $600 bill, a category that did not previously exist

## 3. Three tiers of noncustomers

**Tier 1 — soon-to-be.** People who open the bill, sense something is wrong, search a phrase
from it, hit forum threads and paywalls, and pay anyway. They are actively looking for this
and finding nothing usable. Reached at the moment of doubt: search, and the hospital's own
financial counselor.

**Tier 2 — refusing.** People an advocate turns away. Their bill is $700, not $70,000, and
the contingency fee would not cover the hour. Others refuse on principle to sign over a
third of their own money or hand a stranger their medical records. Small bills are the
majority of bills, and nobody serves them at all.

**Tier 3 — unexplored.** People who never knew a bill could be wrong: uninsured and self-pay
patients, students paying a first ER bill, people billing in a second language, anyone who
pays the day it arrives because a hospital bill reads like a tax notice. Their legal rights —
a good faith estimate, a $400 variance dispute, a collections hold — expire unused. This is
the largest tier and has never been anyone's customer.
